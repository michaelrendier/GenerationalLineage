# Circle — every unit circle, and the seven ways to draw one

*`engine/toolsets/circle.py` · tutorial: [`examples/50_circle.py`](../examples/50_circle.py) · transcript: [`examples/transcripts/50_circle.txt`](../examples/transcripts/50_circle.txt) · added in 1.0*

**Kind of factor:** the circle as the Add:Scale:Sign floor read geometrically — a cataloguing and relating move.

## Definition

A circle is three things: a centre (Add), a radius (Scale) and an orientation (Sign). Give the move a number **and the role it plays** — radius, diameter, circumference or area — and it returns the whole row (r, d, C, A), the reading in units of π, whether the number lies in the exact lattice {2^a·π^b}, its imaginary counterpart (radius iρ: d = 2iρ, C = 2πiρ, A = −πρ²) and its ℓ_p dual. A circle drawn six ways (orbit, unfold, cut, fold, project, shuffle) is the same circle plus a transform that leaves one pathway; the seventh circle changes the NORM instead of the drawing: the ℓ_p unit circle with its own π_p and the p↔q duality.

## Why it belongs in the engine

It is the first-line test for 'is this coincidence structure or just π-algebra': the constants 1/2, 1, 2, π, 2π, 1/π and √2 all lie in {2^a·π^b}, so every near-equality between them is exact algebra; φ, e, ln 10, Ω, d* and the gap lie outside it and produced no cross-constant hit. And it relates equations: the law of sines and the law of cosines keep their form for imaginary sides (they are homogeneous); only the sign of every area flips, which is the Sign component, and a sphere of radius iρ is the hyperbolic plane — area ρ²(π − A − B − C).

## The two directions

| direction | what it does | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **`descend`** (free) | the circle row for any (number, role), the π-unit circles, the six drawings, the ℓ_p circle, and any catalogued value read as a Σ_RB coupling | 0 — a single forward pass, no search |
| **`build_up`** (paid, or refused) | a target measure -> every catalogued constant, in every role, whose circle produces it; cost = candidates tried | reported in `cost`; raises `AscentNotFree` (with `.owed`) when it is asked for something it cannot build from |

**Residual** (what is left when the factor is removed): the Sign: which branch of the square root an area takes.

## Guarantee and how it is checked

27 self-checks, run exactly. r = 1/2 gives d = 1, C = π, A = π/4; C(r = 1/2) = A(r = 1) = π. An imaginary circle has A = −π and C² = 4πA exactly. Under a, b → ia, ib the law of cosines keeps its form (c → ic), the area flips sign, and Heron's 16A² is unchanged. A spherical triangle has angle excess and the same triangle on radius i has the angle deficit, with Gauss–Bonnet area equal to each. The ℓ_p circle gives π₂ = π, π₁ = π_∞ = 4 and π_p = π_q to 1e-5; the SL(2,ℝ) trace test separates elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic.

**Limits, stated plainly:** The cosine form of the spherical law cancels catastrophically for radius ≳ 1e5 (the excess is area/R², below double rounding); the check records this limit instead of hiding it. The Add/Scale/Sign placement of π (C → Add, A → Scale, d → Sign) is a proposal, not a theorem; the three circles are computed either way.
